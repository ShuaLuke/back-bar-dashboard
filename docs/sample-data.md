# The synthetic sample dataset

The tool opens on a made-up dataset so every view works before anything real is loaded. It is generated by `src/sample.js` from a fixed seed, so the files are identical every time; `node scripts/make_sample.js` writes them to `data/sample/`, and the Data tab downloads the same files. **Nothing in it is the restaurants' real cost, sales or margin data.**

## What is real and what is invented

| Real (from the public menus, read Oct 13–14, 2026) | Invented |
|---|---|
| The cocktail and zero-proof lists of Oyamel, Jaleo and Zaytinya: 59 drinks with their names, ingredients and menu prices | Every bottle cost, bottle size and yield |
| The canonical ingredients those drinks use (97 bottles, juices, syrups, garnishes and mixers) | The specs: how much of each ingredient goes in a drink |
| | The eleven call brands and their up-charges, the $12 well & call drink and the Zaytinya $1 cocktail discount |
| | Three months of checks, every well & call order, every food line, the waste log, the bottle counts and the P&L figures |

## Parameters and the ranges behind them

Costs are placed inside ranges a beverage director would recognise, not taken from any distributor. Bottle sizes and units are chosen so the bottle-to-pour math is exercised (750 ml and 1 L bottles, quarts of juice, bitters by the dash, garnish by the piece; yields from 80% to 100%).

| Group | Standard pour | Cost per pour (sample) | Typical published range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Well spirits | 1 fl oz | $0.80–1.30 | $0.60–1.50 |
| Call brands | 1 fl oz | $1.00–2.30 (Don Julio Reposado the dearest) | $0.90–3.00 |
| Mezcal, sotol, absinthe, chartreuse | 1 fl oz | $1.50–2.90 | $1.20–4.00 |
| Liqueurs and amari | 1 fl oz | $0.80–2.90 | $0.60–3.00 |
| Vermouth, sherry, wine, beer | 1 fl oz | $0.18–0.80 | $0.15–1.00 |
| Fresh citrus juice | 1 fl oz | $0.23–0.36 | $0.20–0.45 |
| Fruit juices and purées | 1 fl oz | $0.22–0.60 | $0.20–0.80 |
| Syrups and sweeteners | 1 fl oz | $0.04–0.38 | $0.05–0.50 |
| Mixers | 1 fl oz | $0.06–0.28 | $0.05–0.35 |
| Herbs and garnish | 1 piece | $0.10–0.20 | $0.05–0.40 |

Specs by family: 2 fl oz of the base spirit (1 fl oz each when a drink has two), 0.75 fl oz of a liqueur, 1 fl oz of vermouth or sherry, 2 fl oz of wine, 0.75 fl oz of citrus, 1 fl oz of juice or purée, 0.5 fl oz of a syrup, 3 fl oz of a mixer, 2 dashes of bitters, 1 piece of garnish. Resulting theoretical pour cost: about 20.5% of beverage sales before extras, 23% with extras (ice, garnish and glassware), inside the 18–24% band usually quoted for cocktail bars.

Other parameters (all in `src/sample.js`):

- **Restaurants**: Oyamel (150 drink checks on a Friday, cocktails as priced), Jaleo (170), Zaytinya (140, cocktails $1 cheaper). Weekday/weekend profiles peak Friday and Saturday; July = 1.00, August = 0.93, September = 1.05.
- **Channels**: dine-in 62–66%, bar 26–30%, takeout 2–3%, events 5–6%. Happy-hour checks in the dining room carry a 15% discount 35% of the time; 2% of checks have one comped drink (`net_price` 0).
- **Dayparts**: dinner-heavy in the dining room (62%), happy hour and late night heavier at the bar.
- **Checks**: 1 drink (50%), 2 (32%), 3 (13%), 4 (5%); bar and event checks are larger. 30% of drinks are well & call orders (a spirit plus a mixer, 45% of them calling a brand, 4% doubles, a lime wedge on a third of them), 12% zero-proof where the menu has it, the rest menu cocktails with popularity weights that favour cheaper drinks. 6% of cocktails are made with a called brand (a modifier on a menu drink).
- **Food on the check**: small plates on 82% of dining-room checks (48% at the bar), entrées on 60% (14%), dessert on 20–30%. Three food sections per restaurant with plausible dish names; none of them is costed.
- **Cost versions**: on 2026-09-01 tequila and mezcal +12%, the three agave call brands +10%, lime +15%, lemon +10%, sparkling wine −5%.
- **Extras**: cocktails $0.38 in-house / $0.72 to-go; zero-proof $0.32 / $0.60; well & call $0.22 / $0.55; everything else $0.12.
- **Waste log**: weekly, per restaurant and perishable ingredient, 5–12% of usage for juice, herbs, wine and dairy, 1–1.5% for spirits and liqueurs (spills, comps, breakage), plus a dead-stock floor for four slow bottles (tsipouro, yellow Chartreuse, Mavrodaphne, Velvet Falernum) so they show waste far above their use.
- **Books (actuals)**: beverage cost 8–10% above theoretical by restaurant (a 1.3–2.1 point gap, inside the pass band), except Zaytinya in September at 42% above (a 9.5 point gap: a deliberate Fail).
- **Inventory counts**: twelve bottles counted in tenths of a bottle, with built-in variances (lime +18%, vodka +12%, vermouth +11%, sparkling wine +14%, tequila +9%, Casamigos +3%).

## Size and speed

92 days × 3 restaurants: about 41,000 checks, 156,000 check lines (75,000 drinks and 80,000 food lines) and 55,000 modifier rows. The page builds the dataset in about 0.4 s and analyses it in under a second on a laptop. A real single restaurant-month is a quarter of that. Pass `scale` to `makeSample` for larger tests.

## Regenerating

```
node scripts/make_sample.js
```

writes `data/templates/`, `docs/data-dictionary.md` and `data/sample/`. The two large files (`sales.csv`, `modifiers.csv`) are not committed; one restaurant-week excerpts of each are.
